## Check Token: Real-Time Self-Verification and Precise Truncation in LLM Reasoning

Published: November 16, 2026

An unreviewed arXiv preprint proposes a special “check token” that lets a model trigger self-analysis, error localization, truncation, or rewriting during generation. On Qwen3-32B and Qwen3-Next, the authors report a 10.8-point HMMT25 gain at 1.13× overhead, with benefits increasing as reasoning chains lengthen.

**Why it matters:** The method targets a central weakness of long reasoning chains: errors can propagate before an external verifier inspects the final answer. Its reported overhead is modest, but the evidence is currently limited to unreviewed results on Qwen-based models and mathematics benchmarks.

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18564) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: preprint, reasoning, self-verification, truncation, evaluation

## VidEngram: Agentic Long-Term Video Understanding via Structured Hierarchical Memory

Published: November 16, 2026

VidEngram is an unreviewed agentic video-QA system that builds persistent hierarchical episode and entity memories once during ingestion, then answers later questions through a text-only planner and retrieval tools. The authors report 37.0% on OmniVideoBench versus 32.3% for a single-pass Qwen2.5-Omni baseline, with larger gains on longer videos, and provide an interface for inspecting retrieval traces and evidence.

**Why it matters:** Separating perception from repeated question answering could reduce the cost and coverage loss of long-video analysis. Its hierarchical, inspectable memory design is also relevant to reusable multimodal agents beyond video.

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18578) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: preprint, video understanding, agent memory, multimodal, retrieval

## ScopeBench: Evaluating Cultural Norm Scope Awareness in LLMs

Published: November 16, 2026

ScopeBench is an unreviewed benchmark of 3,323 norms from 49 countries and nine cultural clusters, designed to distinguish universal norms from culture-specific ones. Across 21 frontier models, the authors find large prompt-dependent scope errors: 70% scope deflation in single-answer classification and 33% scope inflation when cultures are judged independently. Models with at least 97% scope-classification accuracy still produced harmful responses to 40–42% of scope-violating statements.

**Why it matters:** The benchmark treats cultural scope as a distinct alignment requirement rather than assuming safety rules are universal. The released data, code, and model outputs provide a concrete basis for testing whether models can both identify whose norms apply and act on that judgment.

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18600) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: preprint, benchmark, safety, cultural scope, dataset

## Understanding Failures in LLM Reasoning by Learning Structured Representations of Chain-of-Thought

Published: November 16, 2026

CoT2Graph converts chain-of-thought traces into directed dependency graphs and uses graph neural networks with attribution to identify structural patterns associated with correct reasoning. The unreviewed paper reports that correct traces emphasize early planning, while failed traces show overloaded synthesis and dependency violations; graph signals outperform prior tree-based methods by up to 2.5% and length baselines by 7.8%, and improve Best-of-N selection.

**Why it matters:** Structural analysis can evaluate reasoning processes that sequence-level accuracy misses, potentially improving both trace diagnosis and selection among multiple generations. The reported results suggest a useful alternative to judging reasoning only by final answers or trace length.

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18611) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: preprint, chain-of-thought, graph neural networks, evaluation, best-of-n

## RGFC: Registry Grounded Function Calling with stage-decomposed evaluation

Published: November 16, 2026

The RFCD benchmark and TAAG evaluator decompose tool-calling failures into registry conformance, schema completeness, and argument grounding across registries containing 5 to 3,000 functions. Tests with six sub-4B models find registry scale to be the main source of degradation; repeated feedback improves some grounding measures but adds substantial token cost and does not consistently improve end-to-end F1.

**Why it matters:** Large tool registries are a realistic bottleneck for production agents, and a single final-call score hides which stage failed. Stage-level diagnostics make tool-use evaluations more actionable while showing that feedback alone does not solve scaling problems.

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18622) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: preprint, tool use, function calling, benchmark, evaluation

## Decision 1.0: Towards Open Decision Foundation Models

Published: November 16, 2026

Decision 1.0 is an unreviewed open-weight family of six encoder and decoder models that turn text and runtime-defined questions into typed probability distributions without autoregressive text generation. Its Choice, Noul, and Score interfaces support composable decisions, while the released Lux-9B model reports 84.17% on explicit decisions and 91.25% on inference, alongside acknowledged gaps in composition, transfer, and calibration.

**Why it matters:** The release offers specialized open models for routing, policy checks, and other reusable control-flow judgments. Typed probabilities and no-generation inference could make decisions cheaper and easier to inspect than free-form LLM outputs, though the benchmark results remain preliminary.

## Minari: Infrastructure for Collaborative Reinforcement Learning Datasets

Published: November 16, 2026

Minari is an unreviewed open-source Python infrastructure for recording, sharing, and loading offline reinforcement-learning datasets through a Gymnasium-style API and Hugging Face Hub. It records environment specifications, seeds, wrappers, and pinned dependencies, and hosts 338 reproducible datasets containing 350,000 trajectories and 55 million steps, including regenerated D4RL data; TorchRL, AgileRL, and d3rlpy support it natively.

**Why it matters:** Standardized, regenerable dataset packaging addresses a persistent reproducibility problem in offline RL. The combination of infrastructure, metadata, hosted data, and library integrations makes the contribution broadly useful beyond its reported baseline experiments.

## Goemans's Unsplittable-Flow Conjecture: The Anatomy of AI-Assisted Counterexample Discovery

Published: November 18, 2026

A paper builds a forward counterexample pipeline for Goemans's unsplittable-flow conjecture, recently disproved by a seven-vertex counterexample found through an interaction with ChatGPT. Using convex-hull conditions over admissible route choices, the authors characterize all counterexamples supported by Rybin's topology and report that ChatGPT, instructed to follow the pipeline, produced new counterexamples starting from 93 other rooted arborescence shapes.

**Why it matters:** It converts a one-off AI-assisted discovery into a repeatable construction method and explains why the original example resisted blind enumeration. This unreviewed preprint is also among the clearer demonstrations of a model applying a specified mathematical procedure to produce novel, verifiable results.

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23244) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: preprint, AI-assisted discovery, mathematics, counterexample construction, theory

## TRACE-MAS: Diagnosing and Repairing Collaboration Failures in Multi-Agent Systems

Published: November 18, 2026

The authors introduce introspective oversight, training a separate third-person observer role inside the model to answer questions about the assistant's own behavior, with answers rewarded when they match what the assistant actually did. An observer trained on Qwen3.6-27B reaches a mean AUROC of 0.907 across five held-out oversight evaluations, compared with 0.864 for honesty training, 0.828 for transcript monitoring and 0.796 for activation oracles; training the observer also makes the assistant easier for baseline monitors to read.

**Why it matters:** Models are generally not trained to report their own misbehavior, so a separately trained, behavior-verified observer offers a plausible alternative to external transcript or activation monitors. Results come from an unreviewed preprint on one model family, and verification against actual behavior presumes access to full traces.

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23362) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: preprint, oversight, interpretability, evaluation, safety

## External sandboxes for coding agents

Published: November 17, 2026

Simon Willison and Prime Radiant describe an external sandbox pattern in which code executed by an agent runs in a dedicated sandbox while the agent itself lives outside it, and released three implementations: a Node.js server using v86 to run Alpine Linux inside WebAssembly, a Python server running the sandbox in Alpine Linux inside an Apple Containers instance, and a Python server bundling Microsoft's Quicksand library (QEMU and Alpine) as a package. Each exposes the same localhost API, with an optional HTTP/HTTPS allow-list proxy and a credential replacement mechanism so authenticated calls can be made without handing secrets to the agent.

**Why it matters:** Executing untrusted code outside the agent's own environment shrinks the blast radius of prompt injection and makes it safer to try local models. The released implementations and shared API make the pattern immediately testable with Pi, Claude Code and Codex.

Source: [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/17/external-sandboxes-for-coding-agents/) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: tooling, sandboxing, security, coding agents, open source

## Prioritize, Don't Decide: What We Learned Auditing a Deployed Human-in-the-Loop CV Sorter

Published: November 18, 2026

TRACE-MAS diagnoses and repairs failures in LLM multi-agent systems by learning normal collaborative execution patterns from benign trajectories, detecting deviations in agent state changes, message transitions and task dependency evolution, then attributing the fault to responsible execution elements and selecting minimal interventions. On five multi-agent benchmarks the authors report a 60.2% improvement in root-cause localization accuracy and consistent gains in task recovery over strong existing safeguards.

**Why it matters:** Current safeguards inspect agents individually, which misses failures that originate in the interactions between them. Interaction-level attribution with minimal repair points toward a more actionable direction for multi-agent reliability, though the reported gains are from an unreviewed preprint.

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23359) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: preprint, multi-agent systems, diagnosis, evaluation, safety

## Training Language Models for Introspective Oversight

Published: November 18, 2026

A paper frames agentic coding with an explicit thermodynamics analogy, counting generated tokens as cost and dividing them into work and heat, with a test suite acting as a software Maxwell's daemon that judges every change. In an experiment where an agent repeatedly makes a repository change and then undoes it, about 80% of the tokens in a successful run are spent in calls that leave the requested change where it was, and the estimated 'free energy' of a change is 14 to 22 percent of the mean cost of a successful coding run.

**Why it matters:** It offers a vocabulary and measurement procedure for asking what tokens actually buy in agentic coding, which matters for cost control and for judging whether long agent runs are productive. The framing is provocative and the evidence comes from a synthetic cycling setup, so practical tooling value is not yet demonstrated.

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23365) · Based on feed excerpt.

## SpecLook: Speculating Where to Look for Fast and Reliable Agentic MLLMs

Published: November 18, 2026

A training-free, reference-guided data curation recipe for 3D vision-language pretraining uses a small high-quality reference set to extract quality signals for each modality of a large uncurated pool, merges them by rank aggregation, and uses the resulting ranking to select training samples. On 35k 3D abdominal CT volume-report pairs from two institutions, the curated model matches the zero-shot disease classification of a state-of-the-art baseline while using 3.3 times less training data and a 5k-sample reference set; on 58k chest X-ray pairs it matches or exceeds a model trained on the full pool using up to 10 times less data and only 74 reference samples, and the code is released.

**Why it matters:** Curation recipes that require no manual labeling could make medical vision-language pretraining considerably cheaper and more reproducible, and the small-reference results suggest most of the filtering value comes from a modest exemplar set. The numbers come from an unreviewed preprint and the zero-shot comparison is against a specific baseline.

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23483) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: preprint, dataset curation, medical imaging, vision-language models, training data

## Physics of Language Models: Part 4.2, Canon Layers at Scale where Synthetic Pretraining Resonates in Reality

Published: November 19, 2026

An unreviewed arXiv preprint reports 64 from-scratch pretraining runs of Transformers and three linear-model families at 1.3–8B parameters and 1–2T tokens. It finds that Canon local-mixing layers can save about 30% of pretraining tokens at equal 8B-model MMLU accuracy, while standard benchmarks conceal weak short-context retrieval and two-hop performance.

**Why it matters:** The controlled study challenges architecture comparisons based only on standard benchmark scores and shows that architectural differences can emerge only at large training scales. Its costly, unreviewed results nevertheless provide unusually broad evidence about scaling and in-context learning.

## Global Importance Estimation for KV Cache Eviction

Published: November 19, 2026

An unreviewed arXiv preprint introduces PRKV, a training-free KV-cache eviction method that applies Personalized PageRank to attention-induced token-transition graphs to estimate multi-hop importance. Across long-context, sparse-retrieval, and chain-of-thought tasks, it reports competitive performance at cache compression ratios up to 90% with low end-to-end overhead.

**Why it matters:** PRKV targets KV-cache memory without modifying the model, offering a practical inference optimization for long-context serving. Its graph-based importance estimate goes beyond recency and instantaneous attention when deciding which cached tokens to retain.

## BuggyMLE: Scaling Machine Learning Engineering Difficulty with Bug Injection

## Forget Freely, Grep Later: Lossless, Index-Free Memory for Long-Horizon LLM Agents

Published: November 19, 2026

## Phantom Success, Silent Failure: Auditing the Success Checkers of Simulated Manipulation Benchmarks

Published: November 19, 2026

An unreviewed arXiv preprint presents SimAudit, a pipeline that compares task instructions with benchmark success-checker code, recreates possible disagreements in simulation, and verifies outcomes through multi-view renderings. Across RoboTwin 2.0, RoboCasa365, and RoboDojo, it identifies 61 defects in 49 tasks, including 40 phantom successes and 21 silent failures.

**Why it matters:** The audit shows that benchmark results can be distorted by success checkers that accept incomplete behavior or reject valid alternatives. SimAudit provides a concrete method for evaluating evaluation infrastructure, not just the policies being tested.
