## The Date You Didn't Set: The System-Prompt Date as a Hidden Variable in LLM Evaluation

Published: November 24, 2026

An unreviewed arXiv preprint finds that automatically inserted system-prompt dates can alter LLM evaluations even when prompts, decoding, and configuration remain fixed. Across six models and four benchmarks, changing between plausible dates switched a median 6.6% of items; optimized dates reduced Llama-3.x accuracy by as much as 36.5 points, altered judge verdicts, and undid 15–32% of reproduced jailbreak successes.

**Why it matters:** The study identifies the system-prompt date as a hidden evaluation variable and recommends pinning, logging, and reporting it to preserve reproducibility and avoid distorted safety results.

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31254) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: unreviewed-preprint, LLM-evaluation, reproducibility, system-prompts, jailbreaks

## Ramanujan Challenge Results: AI-Assisted Evaluation Pipeline for Multi-Format Proofs

Published: November 24, 2026

An unreviewed arXiv preprint analyzes submissions to the Ramanujan Challenge, a research-level mathematics benchmark. Submissions included distinct correct solutions and a proof of one published open conjecture. The authors introduce an evaluation pipeline for natural-language proofs, CAS code, and Lean formalizations, reducing false-positive verdicts on natural-language proofs from 23% to 6% and raising supporting-code judgment accuracy from 46% to 58%.

**Why it matters:** The work highlights both AI-assisted progress in research mathematics and the need for more reliable, multi-format proof evaluation as benchmarks move beyond conventional contest problems.

## Agentic Systems Need Multi-Run Evaluation: A Study of Retrieval and Response Variability in a Deployed Enterprise RAG System

Published: November 24, 2026

An unreviewed arXiv preprint evaluates a deployed enterprise agentic RAG system by running 739 documentation questions ten times each. The correct page appeared in at least one run 69.4% of the time but in all ten runs only 50.9% of the time; retrieval accounted for 43.9% of trace divergence, with the initial number of parallel searches explaining 20.1% of divergent pairs.

**Why it matters:** The results show that single-run testing can conceal substantial retrieval and answer variability, supporting multi-run evaluation for measuring the reliability of agentic RAG systems.

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31266) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: unreviewed-preprint, agentic-RAG, multi-run-evaluation, retrieval, enterprise-AI

## LAION-BVD: A 13-Million-Hour Open Video Dataset for Multimodal Pre-training

Published: November 25, 2026

This unreviewed arXiv preprint introduces LAION-BVD, an open multimodal video dataset built from 1.3 billion platform-specific CommonCrawl URLs. The authors report downloading 80 million videos totaling 13.5 million hours, extracting clips through content-aware scene detection, and generating synthetic video and audio captions. Models trained on the data achieve competitive video-text and audio-text benchmark results, with performance improving as training or model scale increases; scene-changing frames also serve as an alternative image-text source.

**Why it matters:** LAION-BVD could substantially expand access to large-scale open video data for multimodal pre-training and support research across video, audio, and image modalities. Its reported scale and scale-dependent gains make it a potentially important resource, although the preprint has not been peer reviewed.

## PRECEPT: Security Self-Improvement of Coding Agents via Evolving Principles

Published: November 25, 2026

This unreviewed arXiv preprint studies why coding agents fail to retain security lessons: across 14 task sequences, agents that fixed a vulnerability later reintroduced the same vulnerability class in 26.7% of sessions, close to the 27.5% rate among agents never shown the failure. PRECEPT maintains an evolving library of secure-coding principles that is revised in response to the agent’s own failures, with revisions retained only when fresh executions improve security while preserving functional success. On unseen task families, the authors report 97.1% secure working code from the weakest PRECEPT-enabled agent, compared with 81.9% for the strongest unaided agent and 87.7% for the strongest baseline.

**Why it matters:** The work reframes secure coding as a cross-session self-improvement problem and proposes a concrete mechanism for making security knowledge actionable on new code. If independently validated, PRECEPT could reduce recurring vulnerabilities in coding agents beyond what persistent notes or generic principles achieve.

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33519) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: coding-agents, software-security, self-improvement, secure-coding, agent-memory

## Evaluation and Restoration of Safety in Reasoning-Tuned LLMs

Published: November 25, 2026

This unreviewed arXiv preprint defines a safety metric for reasoning-tuned LLLM families and reports that increased reasoning is associated with higher unsafe rates in every tested family. The authors propose ESPG, a post-training guardrail that combines evolution strategies with safety training. They describe it as simple to implement, requiring no GPU memory beyond inference and as few as 30 training samples, while significantly improving model safety.

**Why it matters:** The findings challenge the assumption that stronger reasoning inherently produces safer models and offer a low-resource post-training approach for open reasoning models. The reported safety benefits warrant independent evaluation before being used to characterize or deploy such models.

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33515) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: LLM-safety, reasoning-models, post-training, guardrails, evolution-strategies

## AutoformBench: A Lean Formalization Benchmark for Advanced Mathematics

Published: November 25, 2026

This unreviewed arXiv preprint presents AutoformBench, a Lean 4 benchmark containing 341 formalization tasks drawn from 24 advanced mathematical sources. Experienced mathematicians formalized and audited the target statements, while agents receive the informal proof and must produce the Lean proof, including missing definitions, intermediate lemmas, and supporting infrastructure. Comparisons of frontier models reveal a broadly shared difficulty frontier, with performance depending more on infrastructure construction than on mathematical domain.

**Why it matters:** AutoformBench evaluates realistic mathematical formalization rather than isolated theorem proving, making it useful for measuring whether AI systems can build the supporting theory required for advanced mathematics. Its open data and code may support comparable research, though it remains an unreviewed preprint.

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33165) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: Lean, mathematical-formalization, theorem-proving, AI-benchmark, advanced-mathematics

## Towards Faithful Agentic XAI: A Verification Method and an Open-World Benchmark for Better Model Faithfulness

Published: November 25, 2026

This unreviewed arXiv preprint proposes Faithful Agentic XAI (FAX), which decomposes draft natural-language explanations into claims, queries the target model directly with inherently faithful tools, and removes unsupported or contradicted claims before generating the final explanation. The authors also introduce CRAFTER-XAI-Bench, an open-world reinforcement-learning benchmark for model-specific faithfulness. They report improved simulation faithfulness from 0.20 for the strongest baseline to 0.46 while retaining high informativeness, relevance, and fluency, and find that tabular benchmarks can conflate task accuracy with faithfulness.

**Why it matters:** The work highlights explicit claim verification as a way to prevent plausible but unfaithful agentic explanations and argues that faithful XAI evaluation must test explanations against the target model’s behavior. Both the verification method and benchmark could improve the reliability of model-specific explainability research.

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27879) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: explainable-AI, agentic-XAI, faithfulness, model-verification, reinforcement-learning
