## KernelMosaic: An Open Dataset and Agentic Framework for CUDA Kernel Generation

Published: November 12, 2026

The unreviewed arXiv preprint introduces KernelMosaic, an open dataset pairing diverse CUDA tasks with verified solutions and agent trajectories. Fine-tuning a small open-source model on it raises reported KernelBench L1, L2, and L3 correctness from 4%, 0%, and 0% to 88%, 82%, and 16%; trajectory supervision also outperforms direct-response supervision.

**Why it matters:** It provides a reusable training recipe for code models in systems domains, combining task specifications, executable correctness feedback, and iterative optimization trajectories rather than only final answers.

## Cleaning an Agent's History Hurts When the World Still Holds the Error

Published: November 12, 2026

The unreviewed arXiv preprint separates an agent's recorded history from the actual world state while studying recovery after mistakes. On AgentCE-Bench, a truthful correction note raises receiver recovery from 51% to 66%, while a record of a completed action lowers it from 50% to 44%; AppWorld shows that a success receipt can help or hurt depending on whether the underlying effect exists.

**Why it matters:** The experiments show that cleaning an agent's memory is not inherently beneficial: an edit's value depends on consistency between the recorded narrative and persistent world state.

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14654) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: agents, memory, evaluation, world-state, reliability

## Benchmarking Multilingual Document Understanding in Vision-Language Models

Published: November 12, 2026

The unreviewed arXiv preprint introduces MDU, a benchmark with 23,146 examples, 13 document-understanding tasks, and five languages: English, French, German, Italian, and Spanish. It evaluates 45 open-weight VLMs from 1.7B to 235B parameters plus one commercial system; the best average score is 69.0, and greater model scale does not close language gaps or align the evaluated capabilities.

**Why it matters:** MDU broadens document-AI evaluation beyond English and narrow tasks, exposing uneven multilingual performance and weak relationships among transcription and semantic capabilities.

## Leaderboard Hacking: Preference-Based Model Evaluations are Vulnerable to Manipulation

Published: November 12, 2026

The unreviewed arXiv preprint shows that Bradley–Terry preference leaderboards can be manipulated through strategic voting, prompting, and nomination. On public leaderboard data, each attack can move a model by up to 15–20 places, while combined attacks can move it by up to 45 places.

**Why it matters:** The study identifies a structural vulnerability in arena-style evaluation and motivates safeguards for open prompting, voting, and model submission.

## Abstention Is Not Threshold Compliance: Stated Scoring Rules in Open Language Models

Published: November 12, 2026

An unreviewed arXiv preprint tests whether fully open 3–8B language-model recipes follow an explicit answer-or-abstain probability threshold, using 12 checkpoints from three post-training stages of four recipes and exploratory models up to 32B. At t=0.75, adding an abstention-format instruction raised mean abstention from 20% to 91%, but changing t from 0.5 to 0.9 moved it only -0.8 percentage points; some checkpoints made threshold-inconsistent decisions on up to 40% of items. Post-hoc recalibration behaved differently across the evaluation and shifted TriviaQA-to-PopQA settings.

**Why it matters:** The study separates abstention frequency from actual threshold compliance and shows why evaluations should report abstention rate, threshold responsiveness, and answered items separately. Its calibration results also show that performance on the tuning distribution may not transfer to a shifted one.

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14661) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: evaluation, abstention, calibration, open-models, post-training

## Together Compiler: Continual Compilation for AI Inference

Published: November 12, 2026

An unreviewed arXiv preprint presents Together Compiler, or TKCC, a continual agent loop for optimizing inference workloads with a persistent engine and cost-model-selected parallelism. The authors report up to 1.7× performance over hand-written Megakernels on Llama 1B and up to 2.6× higher tokens per second than open-source engines on four frontier models in InferenceX tests, plus 1,111–1,205 tokens per second at batch size 1 on MiniMax M3.1 Flash and DeepSeek v4.1 Flash. They also say TKCC serves trillions of tokens in production.

**Why it matters:** TKCC addresses a practical deployment problem: models, architectures, and traffic distributions change faster than kernels can be hand-tuned. It makes agent hill-climbing an inference-compilation workflow with concrete throughput benchmarks, although the headline gains remain author-reported on the evaluated systems.

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14681) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: inference, agents, compiler, optimization, throughput, benchmarking

## Trait Hitchhiking in LLM Post-Training

Published: November 12, 2026

An unreviewed arXiv preprint formalizes the evolution of traits during reinforcement-learning post-training with an update expression derived from the Price equation, calling the mechanism trait hitchhiking. Evaluations on language models trained with GRPO show that the Price estimates closely track sampled trait dynamics.

**Why it matters:** The framework gives a mathematical account of how reinforcement learning can amplify or suppress behaviors that the reward does not target. Tracking this quantity could complement task scores when auditing post-training for unintended side effects.

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14683) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: post-training, reinforcement-learning, grpo, evaluation, interpretability, language-models

## What Entropy-Based View Selection Cannot See: An Audit of Test-Time Adaptation for Vision-Language Models

Published: November 12, 2026

An unreviewed arXiv preprint audits entropy-based selection of augmented views in test-time adaptation for vision-language models. It finds that view selection, rather than the gradient update, accounts for almost all accuracy gains; when one view is correct, entropy ranks correct views no better than chance and captures only about one-twentieth of the oracle gain. Ranking crops by retained-image area beats entropy on 14 of 15 datasets and the strongest tested scoring function on 11 of 15.

**Why it matters:** The audit challenges a common confidence heuristic and shows that a simple augmentation signal can outperform it. It also separates preprocessing gains from the claimed benefit of the adaptation update itself.

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14686) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: evaluation, test-time-adaptation, vision-language-models, robustness, ablations

## Code as Explanations: Executable and Verifiable LLM Explanations

Published: November 12, 2026

An unreviewed arXiv preprint proposes code explanations: programs that reproduce an LLM's outputs across a dataset, making behavior-level explanations executable and automatically checkable. Across three open-source code-capable models and eight tasks, the programs were 6–100× shorter than chain-of-thought and aligned better in 9 of 24 settings, but consistency fell on linguistically complex tasks; execution feedback improved results for Claude Opus 5.5 without removing that limitation.

**Why it matters:** Code explanations shift evaluation from plausible per-instance rationales to testable programs over many inputs. The results suggest a compact operational analysis tool, while the complexity-dependent failures show that it is not a general-purpose account of model reasoning.

Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14704) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: interpretability, executable-explanations, evaluation, open-models, code
