## Artificial intelligence and European firms: the cost of market-based financing

Published: November 12, 2026

An ECB Economic Bulletin analysis built a firm-level AI-exposure measure from earnings-call transcripts of STOXX Europe 600 companies. AI-exposed firms earned an equity return premium of about 1.3 percentage points per quarter, paid lower option-adjusted corporate-bond spreads and issued more long-dated debt.

**Why it matters:** The findings indicate that AI exposure is priced across a broad range of European adopters, not just infrastructure providers and hyperscalers. The transcript-based measure also offers a way to identify firm-level AI adoption beyond broad sector labels.

Source: [European Central Bank](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//press/economic-bulletin/focus/2026/html/ecb.ebbox202607_06~a36e367caf.en.html) · Based on feed excerpt.

## The real and financial effects of local corporate tax increases: evidence from linked firm–bank data

Published: November 11, 2026

An ECB working paper links Portuguese corporate balance sheets and income statements to loan-level Bank of Portugal registry data, then uses local-projection difference-in-differences to study municipal corporate surtaxes. Tax increases reduce liquidity, raise borrowing costs and non-performing loans, and lead to lower sales, employment and productivity, with particularly strong effects among small firms.

**Why it matters:** The linked firm-bank design shows how localized tax changes can tighten financing and weaken real activity without creating offsetting gains for neighboring firms, raising questions about fiscal design in decentralized systems.

Source: [European Central Bank](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp3309~4e8af93b3a.en.pdf) · Based on feed excerpt.

Tags: corporate taxation, Portugal, bank lending, firms, productivity

## The impact of monetary policy on productivity

Published: November 15, 2026

An ECB occasional paper uses consistent monetary-policy shock definitions and large-scale euro-area micro-distribution exercises to study productivity. Expansionary shocks improve capital reallocation, market power strengthens transmission, and effects on labour reallocation and zombie firms are more nuanced; results concerning bank characteristics remain inconclusive.

**Why it matters:** The analysis suggests that contractionary monetary-policy shocks may carry productivity costs through weaker resource allocation, while differences in market power complicate how those effects spread through the economy.

Source: [European Central Bank](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpops/ecb.op402.en.pdf) · Based on feed excerpt.

## The macroeconomic effects of defence spending: what impact beyond GDP?

Published: November 12, 2026

An ECB working paper estimates defence-spending effects for up to 27 EU countries from 1999 to 2025 using panel local projections and a SIPRI-based adjustment for the timing of capital expenditure. Capital spending drives positive output effects, while personnel and intermediate consumption generate stronger inflation pressure per euro; average multipliers are lower in high-debt countries.

**Why it matters:** The research looks beyond headline GDP multipliers to show how the composition and timing of defence spending affect output, inflation and fiscal constraints.

Source: [European Central Bank](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp3310~6ef5699d9a.en.pdf) · Based on feed excerpt.

## Narratives and risks: a quantitative toolkit to evaluate scenarios applied to the Middle East conflict

Published: November 12, 2026
